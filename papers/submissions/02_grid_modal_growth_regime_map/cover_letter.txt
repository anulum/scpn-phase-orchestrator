Dear Editor,

Please find enclosed my manuscript entitled:

"A domain-specific modal-growth detector clears a matched false-alarm
bar on power-grid instability, and an eigenvalue regime map shows when
its form transfers"

A companion manuscript establishes that generic early-warning
detectors are statistically at chance under a matched-false-alarm,
permutation-tested protocol on power-grid data (as on brain, heart and
climate corpora). This paper reports the complementary positive result
and bounds it.

A detector that reads the canonical wide-area-monitoring quantity ---
the exponential growth rate sigma of the most unstable bus's cross-bus
voltage-deviation envelope --- leads 36 of 90 generator-trip
transitions of the PSML 23-bus corpus at a matched 10% false alarm
(p = 0.0001; held-out 24 of 45, p = 0.0002), while every generic
detector remains at chance on the identical non-circular split. The
operating point was selected on a development half via a disclosed
variant search and validated on the held-out half.

The result is then stress-tested in four directions: causal streaming
(the deployable operating point recovers 11 of 45 held-out transitions
at a matched stream false alarm), cross-dataset transfer (ISO-NE and
WECC 240-bus; the numeric threshold does not travel on either corpus),
transfer to domains without an exponential signature (scalp EEG and
few-timepoint molecular indices fail), and external validation against
independent ground truths (ANDES small-signal eigenvalues, analytic
normal-form Jacobians, and mean-field Kuramoto eigenvalues). The
validations confirm that the detector estimates the eigenvalue's real
part and yield a regime map: envelope growth on oscillatory modes,
autocorrelation on non-oscillatory ones.

All evidence is content-addressed and byte-reproducible. I believe the
combination of an operational positive result, honest deployment
bounds, and a first-principles regime map will be of interest to the
power-systems and applied-dynamics readership of [Journal Name].

The work is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not
under consideration by another journal.
