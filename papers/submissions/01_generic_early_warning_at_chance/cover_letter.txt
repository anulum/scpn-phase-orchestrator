Dear Editor,

Please find enclosed my manuscript entitled:

"Generic early-warning signals are at chance under a matched
false-alarm protocol across brain, heart, grid, climate and molecular
data"

Most published early-warning signal (EWS) evidence answers a
retrospective, per-record question: is there a statistically
significant rising trend of an indicator within one pre-transition
window relative to surrogates of the same record. This manuscript
addresses the operational question instead: at a fixed false-alarm
budget, does a detector fire on transitions more often than on matched
no-transition controls?

Using one domain-adaptable detector suite and one matched-false-alarm
plus label-permutation protocol, I evaluate four independent labelled
corpora (scalp-EEG seizures, cardiac AF onsets, power-grid growing
oscillations, palaeoclimate abrupt transitions) and a molecular fifth
modality (dynamical-network biomarkers). No detector reaches
significance in any domain. The canonical Dakos et al. (2008)
AR(1)-Kendall-tau detector, run on the identical segments, fares no
better (0 of 6 on its own palaeoclimate records). Because the corpora
are small, the nulls bound demonstrated skill rather than proving
early warning impossible; what they establish is that the operational
bar is materially stricter than the retrospective test on which most
published EWS evidence rests.

All alarms and non-detections are sealed in content-addressed,
byte-reproducible evidence records. A companion manuscript shows that
a domain-specific detector can clear the identical bar where a
deterministic instability signature is present.

I believe the manuscript is of interest to the readership of
[Journal Name] because it supplies a transparent, reusable evaluation
protocol and a multi-domain negative baseline that is currently rare
in the EWS literature.

The work is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not
under consideration by another journal.
